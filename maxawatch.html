MaxaWatch.

A movement tracker designed to disappear — so the watch you already love can stay on your wrist.

01 · Context

A category built by collectors who refused to compromise.

MaxaWatch began with a problem its founders refused to accept. People who love their wristwatches — the pieces that hold meaning, history, taste — were being asked to take them off in exchange for a fitness band. Choose your data, or choose your craft. The two were treated as incompatible.

They aren't. MaxaWatch sits under the wristwatch you already own, invisible against the skin, and counts everything a fitness tracker counts. The watch stays on. The data still arrives. The trade-off, quietly, ends.

02 · The engagement

Naming the position, then building the brand around it.

Our work is a full build — strategy, positioning, naming, narrative, identity system, and guidelines. The product is a strong enough idea on its own; the brand had to be strong enough to carry it without explaining itself every time.

The thesis is restraint. MaxaWatch is a category that doesn't yet have a vocabulary — it isn't a smartwatch, it isn't a fitness band, and it actively refuses to look like either. The brand has to set the terms of how the product is understood, and hold that line through every surface a buyer meets it on.

2.9mm

Total thickness. Titanium casing, wireless charging coil, flat lithium-polymer cell, optical sensor, adhesive layer. Every component accounted for. The brand has to live up to the engineering — which is the part you don't see.
